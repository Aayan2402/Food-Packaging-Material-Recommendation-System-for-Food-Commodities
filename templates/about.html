1. Background & SIH Problem Statement 236

In India, an estimated 16% of fruits and vegetables and over 10% of oilseeds and dairy are lost along the post-harvest supply chain between farm gate and retail consumer. A major driver of this catastrophic waste is sub-optimal, unscientific, or one-size-fits-all packaging: farmers and small-to-medium enterprises (SMEs) routinely package breathing fresh produce into airtight polythene bags (inducing suffocation and fungal mold), or transport fried snacks in single-layer films with zero UV or oxygen barriers.

PackSense AI solves this fundamental challenge by combining food biochemical science, dynamic Indian highway transit climate stress models, and a multi-variable constraint optimization algorithm to output mathematically verified, sustainable packaging recommendations.

2. Food Science & Biochemical Decay Triggers

🍓 Respiration & Transpiration

Fresh fruits continue to respire after harvest (consuming O₂ and producing CO₂). Airtight barriers trigger anaerobic fermentation; excessive moisture leads to water droplet sweating and gray mold (Botrytis).

🥔 Lipid Oxidation & Free Radicals

Snacks with 25-38% free fat develop pungent off-flavors (hexanals) when exposed to oxygen and light. They require metallized barrier films with near-zero OTR and UV blocking.

🧀 High Water Activity (aw > 0.95)

Perishable dairy products (paneer, cheese) and meat are susceptible to psychrotrophic bacterial growth and mold slime. They mandate hermetic EVOH vacuum sealing or active MAP gas.

4. Indian Logistics & Climate Stress Multiplier

Standard laboratory testing assumes steady 23°C and 50% relative humidity. In India, however, a truck travelling from Pune to Chennai during the monsoon experiences 88% relative humidity and 36°C ambient heat for 4 days on open highways. PackSense AI computes a Dynamic Climate Stress Score using regional meteorological databases and transit shock estimates to automatically tighten moisture and oxygen barrier targets.
